# UX inspiration research: Career Signal

Date: 2026-09-02

## Executive direction

The useful gap in the market is not another enormous career test and not another decorative personality quiz. It is a short, memorable discovery ritual that earns the student's trust, then becomes an unusually practical recruiting guide.

For this product, the recommended split is:

- **Assessment:** focused and cinematic, with one meaningful decision at a time.
- **Reveal:** emotionally satisfying, but modest about what eight questions can prove.
- **Career workspace:** editorial, evidence-rich, and relentlessly useful.
- **Interview practice:** active rehearsal with immediate, understandable feedback.

The proposed visual blend is **BYU field guide meets enchanted signal room**: BYU navy, royal, white, condensed athletic typography, technical grids, monumental letterforms, and sharp editorial panels; then grain, darkness, symbolic illustrations, small points of warm light, and a brief sense of ceremony at important transitions.

It should not resemble a Hogwarts reskin. All symbols, illustrations, names, and motion should be original to the four Information Systems paths.

## What the market suggests

### 1. The result must become useful immediately

The emotional reveal should last seconds, not minutes. Its next screen should answer:

1. Why did this path rise to the top?
2. What does the work actually feel like?
3. What might frustrate me about it?
4. What can I do at BYU this week to test the fit?

Career Signal can outperform broader tools by knowing the exact context: an Information Systems junior preparing to recruit.

### 2. A match needs an explanation, not just a number

The result should display three or four plain-language factors, for example:

- **Problems you notice:** hidden risk and unexplained failures
- **How you begin:** investigate before acting
- **What satisfies you:** catching what others missed
- **Work style signal:** depth and vigilance

The product should call these “signals” or “evidence,” not proof. A close runner-up should remain visible.

### 3. Realistic previews are more valuable than extra description

Each path should eventually include one brief “try the work” card:

- Software development: choose the best debugging next step.
- Data and analytics: decide what a small chart does and does not support.
- Cybersecurity: triage a suspicious access pattern.
- Product and project: resolve a priority conflict with incomplete information.

This is the most promising post-MVP addition because it turns career curiosity into observable evidence.

### 4. Interview practice should feel like rehearsal

The current typed-or-spoken answer model is sound. Its stronger presentation is a rehearsal studio rather than a chatbot:

- one role-specific prompt;
- an optional 30-second thinking period;
- a visible recording state and simple waveform;
- replay/transcript;
- separate cards for answer structure, evidence, role language, and delivery;
- one suggested retry, with progress shown across attempts.

Voice must remain optional and the complete workflow must work with typing. Microphone permission should be requested only after a student intentionally chooses voice.

### 5. Ceremony works when it marks real progress

The Sorting Ceremony references are effective because they make ordinary taps feel consequential. The useful techniques are:

- one focal illustration or choice group;
- very generous negative space;
- brief transitions between questions;
- ambient grain and restrained points of light;
- a recognizable sound control;
- a distinct pre-reveal pause;
- a result that changes the world of the interface.

The lesson is not to cover every surface in magic. The quieter screens make the reveal feel special.

## Proposed visual grammar

### Foundation: BYU sports editorial system

The supplied 2025–2026 references consistently use:

- cobalt or royal blue fields against deep navy;
- hard rectangular crops and architectural alignment;
- thin construction-grid lines;
- enormous condensed headlines and numerals;
- small, widely tracked uppercase metadata;
- grain, directional blur, translucent bands, and photographic depth;
- strong asymmetry and a single dominant subject;
- white utility panels when information density matters.

These ingredients should control layout, hierarchy, and pace.

### Atmosphere: original mystical signal system

The fantasy layer should use:

- four original path sigils, built from IS concepts rather than animals or houses;
- an abstract compass, aperture, constellation, or signal receiver as the central object;
- grainy editorial illustrations with a narrow light source;
- subtle celestial or cartographic markings inside the sports grid;
- warm light used only at moments of discovery;
- environmental sound only if it is opt-in, immediately mutable, and truly improves the experience.

This layer should control meaning and ceremony, not basic navigation.

### Color roles

- **BYU navy `#002E5D`:** structural brand color and dark utility surfaces.
- **BYU royal `#0047BA`:** energy, active states, and athletic editorial fields.
- **White:** text, breathing room, and information-heavy result panels.
- **Mist/light blue:** quiet borders, secondary diagrams, and inactive steps.
- **Warm signal light:** a limited amber or sand accent for discovery, focus, and progress. It should not become a competing institutional color.
- **Path colors:** if retained, keep them mostly inside charts, sigils, and result details rather than allowing four bright app themes to compete with BYU blue.

The [official BYU color guidance](https://brand.byu.edu/colors) identifies navy and white as primary, with royal as the energetic athletic/marketing color. The [web guidance](https://brand.byu.edu/web-theme) recommends navy, white, and royal as dominant website colors and IBM Plex Sans for web typography.

### Typography

- **Athletic display:** a licensed or open-source condensed sans for monumental headlines, path names, question numbers, and score moments.
- **Utility:** IBM Plex Sans for controls, body copy, labels, and accessible reading.
- **Reflective accent:** a serif used sparingly for a short question, line of encouragement, or result interpretation.

This is a three-role system, not three competing fonts. The condensed face creates impact; Plex carries the product; the serif adds humanity.

### Shape and texture

- Mostly square corners or very small radii.
- Hairline grids and frame rules.
- Large crops that can extend beyond the viewport.
- Grain and blue photographic texture behind display moments.
- Clean white or navy reading surfaces behind dense content.
- Avoid generic gradient blobs, glassmorphism everywhere, glowing card borders, and floating 3D objects with no meaning.

## Proposed experience

### 1. Entry: “Find your signal”

A monumental `IS` or four-part signal compass sits behind the composition. One quadrant is sharply illuminated while the others remain barely visible. A short line explains the promise and the start button is unmistakable.

### 2. Assessment: eight scenes, one rhythm

Keep one question per view. Alternate among:

- two illustrated choices;
- four compact symbolic choices;
- one large scene with four labeled responses;
- text-only choices when the wording is the focus.

Display progress as editorial metadata (`03 / 08`) and preserve a reliable Back action. Selection should create an immediate tactile response, but the next question should not advance so quickly that students cannot correct a mistaken tap.

### 3. Pre-reveal: “Your pattern is taking shape”

A brief 1.5–2 second composition assembles the four path signals. The leading one resolves into focus. This should be skippable under reduced-motion settings and should never conceal loading.

### 4. Result: a field guide, not a verdict

The top screen should show:

- primary signal and real career path;
- one-sentence interpretation;
- close runner-up;
- three pieces of answer evidence;
- `Explore the work` and `Build my plan` actions.

Below it, present “The work,” “The recruiting path,” and “Your next seven days” as distinct layers. Long research belongs behind disclosure controls or tabs.

### 5. BYU readiness: a training card

Borrow the ROC schedule's clarity: hard rows, condensed milestones, obvious status, and dates or sequences. Show no more than three urgent steps before “See the full plan.” The student should always know which action matters next.

### 6. Interview practice: a rehearsal room

Use a darker, calmer surface with one prompt and one recording control. The recording state can transform the path sigil into a waveform. Feedback should appear on a clean white or navy field so it feels analytical rather than mystical.

## Graphic and icon opportunities

### Priority 1: useful identity assets

1. **Four path sigils**
   - Builder: interlocking brackets becoming a bridge or working frame.
   - Analyst: a lens revealing a data constellation.
   - Defender: layered boundary lines closing around a protected signal.
   - Orchestrator: four routes converging through a compass point.
2. **Central signal compass** for the home, progress, and reveal states.
3. **One result crest template** that can be recolored/recomposed for each path.
4. **A small role-icon set** for work, skills, pressure, people, tools, and preparation.

### Priority 2: assessment scenes

Create original illustrations for the most visual prompts: restaurant choice, free Saturday, board game, group project, ten-step process, LEGO pieces, and the four satisfaction outcomes. The style should be geometric, grainy, high-contrast, and editorial—not whimsical clip art.

### Priority 3: functional motion assets

- Selection pulse and line-trace.
- Signal particles that subtly reorganize after each answer without revealing the scoring.
- Path sigil draw-on animation.
- Interview waveform and completion stamp.

Generated raster art should be reserved for atmospheric scenes. Core path icons should be SVG so they remain crisp, recolorable, accessible, and fast.

## Original copy principles

The requested voice can be translated into an original style with these characteristics:

- warmly intelligent rather than grand;
- direct second-person address;
- gently encouraging without false certainty;
- occasional understated wit;
- hopeful reframing of uncertainty;
- short vivid examples followed by plain guidance;
- British spelling only if the whole product adopts it consistently;
- no imitation of a living speaker's signature phrases or cadence.

Sample direction:

> Your future need not arrive as a thunderclap. Sometimes it begins with noticing the sort of problem you cannot resist solving.

> There is no clever answer here. Choose the one that feels most like you on an ordinary Saturday.

> A clear pattern is beginning to show. Not a verdict, thankfully—just a rather promising place to begin.

> You seem happiest when scattered evidence begins to tell the truth. That is a useful instinct, and one worth testing in the real work.

> You do not need to prepare for everything at once. Begin with the next honest step; we have put it just below.

The religious character should come through primarily as welcome, dignity, hope, and joy unless explicit faith language is approved for this specific product and audience.

## Interaction guardrails

- One question per page, consistent with the [GOV.UK question-page pattern](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/).
- Always show Back and preserve the browser Back state.
- Use one overall progress indicator rather than several competing indicators, consistent with [Material guidance](https://m2.material.io/components/progress-indicators).
- Never auto-play sound.
- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`; W3C specifically recommends suppressing non-essential interaction motion for users who request it ([Technique C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Techniques/css/C39.html)).
- Do not use color alone to distinguish the four paths.
- Keep body copy on quiet surfaces, even when the surrounding composition is textural.
- Do not obscure the result methodology or call the quiz scientific.
- Treat microphone recording and storage choices as explicit, visible user decisions.

## Recommended next design pass

After the remaining brand decisions are answered:

1. Replace the current generic display system with the condensed/Plex/serif hierarchy.
2. Build the central signal compass and four SVG path sigils.
3. Redesign the home, one quiz question, the reveal, and the top of the result as the visual pilot.
4. Generate two atmospheric assessment illustrations and test whether they add clarity or merely decoration.
5. Apply the resulting system to the full quiz, readiness plan, and interview studio.
6. Test the complete journey on a laptop and phone with reduced motion, keyboard navigation, and no microphone permission.
